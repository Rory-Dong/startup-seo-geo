# Startup website foundation

Use this reference when a company is clarifying its first website, homepage, product pages, or initial organic-growth system.

## Product-to-page framing

Establish:

- primary audience and the painful job they need done;
- category and alternatives the audience already considers;
- product promise, proof, constraints, and first conversion;
- geography, language, compliance, and trust requirements;
- what the company can credibly demonstrate today.

Do not create a large site map before these choices are clear. Start with the smallest useful set of pages, then expand when search demand, customer questions, or product scope justify it.

## Minimum useful site

Choose only the pages the business can support with distinct value:

- Home: category, audience, problem, outcome, proof, and next action.
- Product/service: how it works, who it is for, limits, and evidence.
- Use case or audience pages: only when the workflow and value genuinely differ.
- Pricing or conversion page: current commercial or signup path.
- Resources/docs: explanations that answer real questions and support evaluation.
- About/trust/contact: ownership, expertise, support, and risk-reducing context.

Every page should have one primary intent, one primary next action, and a clear internal-link role.

## Landing-page quality gates

- The first viewport identifies the product, audience, problem, and next action.
- Claims are specific and supported; placeholders are visibly marked.
- Sections follow the user's decision sequence: understand, believe, evaluate, act.
- Mobile layout, text density, keyboard focus, contrast, loading, error, and empty states are usable.
- The page has a stable URL, title, description, H1, canonical, internal links, and appropriate structured data.
- The visual system fits the category and avoids generic decorative patterns that add no decision value.

## Startup prioritization

Prefer one high-quality page that can earn trust and conversions over many thin pages. Rank page ideas by customer value, search intent, proof available, implementation cost, and ability to measure a meaningful outcome.
